# Android app

Install, use and build the PrivatePaste Android app.

## Android app

A sideloaded Android app (Android 10 or newer) that puts clips from your other devices on the phone's clipboard, sends the phone's clipboard or a shared item to them, and shows the searchable history. It talks only to your server: no Google Play, no push service, no analytics.

- **Install**: download `PrivatePaste-android-<version>.apk` from the latest GitHub Release on the phone, open it, and allow "Install unknown apps" for the browser or file manager you used when Android asks.
- **Sign in**: enter the server URL (`https://` only; plain `http://` works only for localhost, for development), your name, your password and a device name (on a new server the owner is `owner`, with the server's `PP_PASSWORD`). The app remembers the name. A wrong name or password shows "Wrong name or password." After that the app stays signed in.
- **Unlock**: clips are end-to-end encrypted (see [Self-hosting](self-hosting.md#end-to-end-encryption)), so after signing in the app shows "Unlock your history" with two ways in:
  - **Passphrase**: enter it and tap Unlock. A wrong one shows "That passphrase doesn't unlock this server's history."
  - **Scan a code**: on a device that is already unlocked, open **Add a device** (Settings in the desktop app, this app or the web UI) and point the camera at the code. Android asks for the camera the first time. A code works once, for 10 minutes; an old one shows "That code has expired. Show a new one."

  The key is then kept in the phone's Android Keystore-protected storage, so the app doesn't ask again. On a server where encryption isn't set up yet, the owner's phone shows "Set up encryption" (choose a passphrase twice: "Use at least 12 characters. A short sentence works well."); anyone else's shows "Ask the owner to set up encryption." and doesn't sync until then. While the app is locked, its notification says "Open PrivatePaste to unlock".
- **Settings > Encryption**: **Change passphrase** (owner only; the history stays and unlocked devices keep working) and **Add a device**, which shows a QR code for another phone to scan ("Scan this with PrivatePaste on your phone. It works once, for 10 minutes.", with "Show a new code" when it runs out). Resetting encryption is in the web UI only.
- **After a reset**: when the owner resets encryption in the web UI, the app clears its history and unsent clips and asks for the new passphrase with "Encryption was reset. Enter the new passphrase."
- **Updates needed**: on a server older than the app it shows "Update your PrivatePaste server to keep syncing."; on a server newer than the app, "Update PrivatePaste to keep syncing." A clip of a kind this version doesn't know (from a newer app) shows "Update PrivatePaste to see this clip" and is never put on the clipboard.
- **Signing out** removes the key from the phone along with the sign-in, and so does the phone being signed out from the People page.
- **Allow notifications and background use**: after the first sign-in the app asks for the notification permission (the service shows a permanent "PrivatePaste connected" notification) and to be exempt from battery optimization. Allow both. Without them Android stops the connection when the phone sleeps.
- **Send clipboard tile**: pull down Quick Settings, tap the edit (pencil) button, and drag "Send clipboard" into the active tiles. Tapping it sends what is on the clipboard now.
- **Share to PrivatePaste**: in any app, use Share and pick "Send to PrivatePaste" to send text, a link, an image or files. One shared picture is sent as an image; any other file, or several items at once, are sent together as one file clip (see [Files](#files)).
- **History**: the app lists the latest 200 clips with search. Search runs on the phone, over the clips it keeps; when they have no more matches, "Search older clips" looks through the rest of the history on the server, decrypting it on the phone. Tap a clip to put it on the clipboard. Long-press a clip for a menu with Pin (or Unpin) and Delete, plus Save and Share on a file clip. Deleting asks first, and the clip disappears on your other devices too.
- **Filter by type**: the chips All, Text, Links, Images and Files under the tabs show only clips of one kind, together with the tab and the search. Tapping the active chip again goes back to All. The filter stays while the app runs (turning the phone, opening Settings) and starts at All when the app is opened afresh. When fewer than 20 clips match, the app loads older clips from the server by itself until it has more or the history ends; with none it says "No files yet" (or "No text yet", "No links yet", "No images yet"; "No pinned files" and so on in Pinned).
- **Pinned**: the Pinned tab lists the clips you pinned on any device, most recently pinned first, even ones older than the 200 in History. Pinned clips also show a pin in History. Pinning doesn't move a clip or put it on the clipboard. Pinned clips are kept when the history is cleared or auto-deleted on the server (both are set in the web UI).
- **Private copies**: the Android app only reads the clipboard when you send it (the tile, Share), so it sends what you chose, even from a password manager. The desktop apps are the ones that skip private copies.
- **Delivery** (Settings): "Copy automatically" writes incoming clips straight to the clipboard and is the default. Android may refuse clipboard writes from a background app, mostly while the phone is locked or another app is in front. If clips from your other devices don't land on the clipboard in that case, switch to "Show a notification with a Copy button": each incoming clip posts a notification, and tapping Copy puts it on the clipboard.
- **Files**: see [Files](#files).
- **Battery settings (Xiaomi, Samsung, Huawei and others)**: these phones stop background apps more aggressively than stock Android. Open Settings → Apps → PrivatePaste → Battery and choose "Unrestricted" (the wording differs by maker). Also:
  - **Xiaomi (MIUI / HyperOS)**: set Battery saver to "No restrictions" for the app and turn on Autostart for it in Security or Permissions.
  - **Samsung (One UI)**: remove PrivatePaste from "Sleeping apps" and "Deep sleeping apps" (Settings → Battery → Background usage limits).
  - **Huawei (EMUI / HarmonyOS)**: in the app's Battery settings turn off "Manage automatically" and allow Auto-launch, Secondary launch and Run in background.
- **Revoked device**: if the phone is signed out from the People page of the web UI (or its person is removed or has their password reset), the app shows a "Signed out" notification and stops. Sign in again to resume.
- **Updating**: install a newer APK over the old one. Android only accepts it when it is signed with the same key as the installed app, so keep using the release APKs from this repository's Releases. The first time you switch between a debug build and a release build you must uninstall the old one.
- **Release** (for the repository owner): run `powershell -File scripts/android-keystore.ps1`. It creates `android/keystore/release.jks` (git-ignored) and prints the password once and the `gh secret set` commands for the four secrets `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS` and `ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD`. Run them, then push a tag such as `android-v0.1.0`. `.github/workflows/android.yml` tests, builds the signed APK and attaches it to a GitHub Release (a version with a `-`, like `0.2.0-rc1`, becomes a prerelease). Back up `release.jks`: without it, updates can't be installed over the existing app.
- **Build it yourself**: in `android/`, run `./gradlew assembleDebug`. `./gradlew assembleRelease` signs only when `PP_ANDROID_KEYSTORE` (path), `PP_ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `PP_ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS` and `PP_ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD` are set; otherwise it produces an unsigned APK and warns. `-PppVersion=0.1.0 -PppVersionCode=1` set the version.
- **End-to-end test**: with a local server running (see [Local development](self-hosting.md#local-development), `PP_PASSWORD=dev`, signed in as `owner`, e.g. `--no-launch-profile --urls http://localhost:5299`), run `PP_E2E_URL=http://localhost:5299 ./gradlew testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LocalServerE2eTest*'` in `android/`. Without `PP_E2E_URL` the test is skipped. Give the server a fresh data directory: the test sets up encryption with its own passphrase and resets it, which deletes the history. It also shares two files from the phone and checks that another client downloads the same bytes. The run signs in five times, the server's limit per minute, so wait a minute before running it again.

## Files

Files go both ways, end-to-end encrypted like every clip: the server never sees their names or contents.

- **Sending**: share files to "Send to PrivatePaste" from Files, Photos (several picked) or any other app. They are sent as one clip, up to 100 files and 25 MB in total (the server's `PP_MAX_FILE_MB`). When nothing is sent, the app says why:
  - "Too many files. Share at most 100 at once."
  - "Files over 25 MB aren't synced." (with the server's limit)
  - "Update your PrivatePaste server to sync files." on a server that doesn't take files.
  - "Couldn't send the files. Share them again when you're online." Unlike text and images, files are never queued to be sent later.
  - "Open PrivatePaste to unlock, then share the files again." while the app is locked.
  - "Couldn't read the files" when the sharing app doesn't hand them over.
- **Receiving**: a file clip from another device is never downloaded on arrival, so a big file doesn't use your data until you want it. It posts a notification that reads like `report.pdf +2 more · 3.4 MB`, with **Save** ("Copy automatically" delivery) or **Copy** and **Save** ("Show a notification with a Copy button"). Save downloads the files into Downloads and says "Saved to Downloads" (or "Couldn't save the files"); a name that is already there gets ` (2)`. Copy puts the files on the clipboard.
- **In the history**: a file row has a file icon and reads like `report.pdf +2 more · 3.4 MB`; search matches every file name. Tap it to copy the files, or long-press for **Save** (to Downloads) and **Share** (to another app). Each downloads the files first if they aren't on the phone yet; without a connection it says "Files need a connection to download".
- **Names from other devices** are made safe before anything is written (folder parts, characters other systems don't allow and reserved names like `CON` are dropped or changed, and a name is cut to 200 bytes). Two files with the same name become `report.pdf` and `report (2).pdf`. A download that fails or doesn't decrypt leaves no file behind.
- **Where they are kept**: downloaded files stay in the app's cache, so Copy and Share work again offline, until their clip leaves the history on the phone, you sign out, or encryption is reset. Android may also clear the cache when storage runs low; they are downloaded again when needed. Files you save to Downloads are yours to keep.
